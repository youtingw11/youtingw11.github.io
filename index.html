About Me

Mathematician at the Top of the World

I am a first-year PhD student in the Department of Applied Mathematics and Applied Physics at Columbia University, co-advised by Lorenzo Polvani and Michael Previdi. My research focuses on understanding the mechanisms driving Arctic amplification and the potential for nonlinear tipping points in the polar climate system.

Before Columbia, I completed a double bachelor's degree from National Taiwan University in Mechanical Engineering and Atmospheric Sciences, graduating with a GPA of 3.96/4.3 and ranking 1st in the Atmospheric Sciences department.

Outside the lab, I enjoy photography, travel, and exploring the intersection of mathematics and the natural world. I speak Mandarin (native), English (fluent), and am learning German.
