hi there!

I am currently a second-year Ph.D. student in Applied Physics and Applied Mathematics at Columbia University, working with Professor Lorenzo Polvani and Professor Michael Previdi. I earned dual bachelor's degrees from National Taiwan University, majoring in Mechanical Engineering and Atmospheric Sciences.

My research interests focus on polar climate change, including causes of Arctic amplification, climate feedbacks in perspective of the energy budget, and Arctic climate under different forcing agents. I also aim to understand climate modeling and variability in projections.

In my leisure, I enjoy playing guitar and drums, and I'm a Man City fan.

Polar Amplification

The Arctic warms 3–4× faster than the global average. I study the mechanisms behind this amplification and how different forcing agents — anthropogenic aerosols, CO₂, non-CO₂ greenhouse gases — produce distinct patterns of Arctic warming.

🌊figure / gif

Sea Ice Loss & Forcings

Arctic sea ice has declined dramatically in recent decades. My research examines how anthropogenic aerosols, greenhouse gases, and ozone-depleting substances each contribute to sea ice loss and the seasonality of these changes.

📈figure / gif

Arctic Climate Tipping Points

I investigate whether the Arctic climate system may cross irreversible tipping points under delayed CO₂ mitigation scenarios, using tools from nonlinear dynamical systems theory and large-ensemble climate simulations.
